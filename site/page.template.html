Noticed,
then chosen.

Every community pull request ever opened against Omarchy, one dot each, stacked by the week it arrived and coloured by what happened to it.

Hover a dot to see the PR, click to pin it with a link.

Most pull requests never hear from a maintainer.

Of the 3,671 community PRs that have been decided, 85% were merged or closed without a single maintainer comment, review or label beforehand. Acceptance mostly arrives the same way. 72% of the 940 accepted PRs were merged straight away, with nothing said first.

That changes what a contributor is optimising for. You rarely get to argue your case. The PR has to make it on its own, at the moment a maintainer finally opens it.

Fixes beat features in every era.

Each community PR was read and labelled by kind (bug fix, feature, security, performance, refactor, docs, packaging) and by the area it touches. Kind turns out to matter more than area.

Features land 12 points less often than bug fixes. The gap opens at the second step. A feature is about as likely to be looked at (−1.8 points), and 12 points less likely to be accepted once it is. The sign holds in 1.x, in 3.0 and in Quattro.

In the Quattro era, the PRs that still get through are security fixes (25%) and performance work (30%), against 9% for bug fixes and 5% for features. Security PRs are also the one kind that reliably pulls a maintainer in: +21 points on attention.

Some things buy a look. Others win the decision.

Splitting the funnel into two steps shows where each feature acts. Getting noticed is a maintainer commenting, reviewing or labelling within 14 days. Getting chosen is acceptance once they have.

A bigger change gets a little more attention and is less likely to land. Touching test/ is the strongest sign at the choosing step. A track record helps, and a first PR costs about five points.

Presentation barely moves anything. Headings, linked issues, measurements and fix: prefixes are flat or slightly negative once everything else is held equal. Saying a PR was written with AI carries no penalty.

When a maintainer asks, answer with commits.

Of 422 decided PRs that got substantive maintainer feedback, 55% were accepted when the author pushed new commits, 23% when the author only replied, and 31% when the author did nothing. Holding other things equal, pushing commits is worth about +27 points.

Speed helps too. PRs answered within six hours were accepted 45% of the time, against 34% for six to 24 hours.

Read this with care. Accepted PRs were decided a median 9 hours after the author's last move, rejected ones 130 hours. Part of the effect is a maintainer who has already decided to merge asking for one last change.

Almost all of this comes from 1.x and 3.0. Since Quattro, only 23 community PRs have received substantive feedback at all.

What to submit, how, when, and what to do once someone looks.

Each finding is tagged with the step it acts on, whether it is in your control, how stable it is across releases, and how likely it is to be a consequence of triage rather than a cause.
